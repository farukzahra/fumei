# Fake or suspicious employer heuristics

Heuristic scoring only — not legal or financial advice. Use **risk: low | medium | high** plus short reasons.

## High risk (prefer skip)

- Posting asks for payment, training fee, equipment purchase, or crypto deposit
- "Interview" via Telegram/WhatsApp only; no company domain email
- Company name unknown + no website + generic Gmail contact for "HR"
- Same job reposted dozens of times with different company names
- Salary far above market for minimal requirements (e.g. $200k USD for entry-level data entry remote)
- Description is mostly keyword stuffing with no product, team, or mission detail
- Job URL or company link goes to unrelated site, parked domain, or social profile only
- Requests copy of ID / bank details before interview
- Multi-level marketing language: "be your own boss", "unlimited income", "work from phone"

## Medium risk (verify before applying)

- Company LinkedIn page created recently with very few employees
- No careers page; apply only through third-party form with excessive personal data
- Recruiter profile: new account, no photo, sparse history
- Job description copied from other listings (search a unique sentence — duplicates elsewhere)
- "International company" with no named product, clients, or engineering blog
- Vague role: "Remote Admin Assistant" + wide skill list unrelated to title
- Uses only premium LinkedIn features to hide company details

## Low risk (still check once)

- Known public company or well-funded startup with verifiable website
- Consistent branding across LinkedIn, careers site, and Glassdoor/Levels presence
- Specific team, stack, and interview process described
- Recruiter from `@company.com` email domain matching careers site

## Quick verification checklist (browser)

When medium/high risk and job otherwise matches:

1. Open company LinkedIn page → employees, founded, about, website link
2. Open company website → careers page, product, contact
3. Search `{company name} scam` — read with skepticism, note patterns
4. Check if same role exists on official careers site (URL match)

Record findings in report; do not accuse — use "elevated risk because …".

## Resume-specific note (Brazil → US remote)

Legitimate US employers often require work authorization. Absence of sponsorship language is **not** fake — it is **incompatible**, not fraudulent. Classify as **Skip (geo)** not **Skip (scam)**.
